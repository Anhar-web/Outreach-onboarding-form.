# Audience Research: her behaviors, her moments, her questions

Step 3, batch of 10 carousels. Builds on `agency-run/research.md` (read that for stigma, cost, "شفاء" programs, competitor map). This file adds: observable behaviors, the exact recurring moments, and the practical questions mothers ask.

**Method and honesty.** About 33 searches (Arabic and English). **Page fetching was blocked again**: hawaaworld.com, mumsnet.com, community.autism.org.uk and the Reddit mirror all failed DNS. Each needs to be allowed in the session's network settings before anyone can verify these quotes in context. Everything below comes from search snippets, page titles or URL slugs. The best Arabic source turned out to be **Altibbi and Islamweb question titles**: their URL slugs hold the parent's own question text, often with typos and the numbers stripped out. **Saudi-dialect speech is still thin.** Most Arabic hits are MSA questions or press articles. Gulf dialect lives in TikTok, Snap and WhatsApp, which search does not index.
Tags: **[REAL]** = verbatim snippet text. **[TITLE]** = a real thread or question title (or URL slug). **[PARAPHRASE]** = the source's content in my words.

## 2. Behavior map: the six areas

**Sleep (النوم)**
- She has to lie beside him until he falls asleep (#12). The "calming" routine (bath, music, a story) winds him up instead (#13).
- He wakes at the same hour every night, screaming or frightened (#6, #12). He's awake late into the night (prior run: "2/3am").
- The moment: **the minute she gets up from beside him**, and **10:30pm or 3am waking**.

**Outings and visits (الخروج والزيارات)**
- Moments: **leaving the park**, **getting out of the car**, **buckling the car seat** (NAS "every trip in the car" meltdowns, PARAPHRASE), **arriving at a family gathering** (#27), **the barber** (alkafeel thread title «لماذا يبكي الطفل عند حلاقة شعره ؟؟»), **the supermarket or mall** with people staring (Asharq Al-Awsat title «نظرات الناس لا تهم…كيف تتعامل مع نوبة غضب طفلك بالأماكن العامة؟»).
- Saudi framing: the Thursday or Eid family visit. Skipping it reads as cutting family ties (#28), and going means people's looks (#9, #10).

**Communication (التواصل)**
- **Doesn't respond to his name but hears everything else.** This is the most common question title on Mumsnet (#25). He understands but doesn't speak (#1). He used to say words and stopped (#2), which is a safety flag to send her to a doctor, never a topic to coach on.
- Moments: **calling him from across the room while he's absorbed**, and guessing what he wants.

**Food (الأكل)**
- Same food, same brand, same plate or cup; beige or yellow foods; foods must not touch (#15–18, Solihull SEND guide).
- Runs from the table (#5). She asks about tests and whether the cause is physical (Islamweb PARAPHRASE).
- Moments: **the dinner plate with his two safe foods**, **the family table at a visit** where relatives push him to eat.

**Transitions (الانتقال / إيقاف شي يحبه)**
- **The second the iPad is taken** (#19–21), **leaving the park** (#24), **shoes and the car in the morning**.
- A five-minute warning often isn't enough (prior run). Parents argue over whose fault it is (#21).

**Emotions and anger (الغضب والانفعال)**
- Screams "over the smallest thing" (#3). **Hits when told no** (the question was searched; the alukah titles in #8 confirm the theme). Hits a sibling (#8). Hits his own head (Arabic Post title «يصرخ ويضرب رأسه في الحائط»). This needs medical referral wording.
- The mother's own anger is part of the scene (#7). Content must hold her without blaming her.

**Sound (cuts across outings and emotions):** NHS leaflets list the triggers: **vacuum cleaner, hand dryers, sirens, bells, fireworks**. Children "display their dislike of sounds physically… by covering their ears or crying" (sfh-tr.nhs.uk, esht.nhs.uk). This is not a mother's voice, but it gives us concrete moments: the hand dryer in the mall toilet, the vacuum, Eid fireworks, wedding speakers.

## 4. What she's tried and dropped

- **Timers, warnings, sticker charts** for transitions. Vendor material says they work; parents say warnings "don't register". No forum post explaining why they fail came through. [PARAPHRASE] Likely reason (synthesis): they're used inconsistently and not tied to *which* moment is hard.
- **The standard bedtime routine** (bath, music, story) makes him more wound up (#13).
- **Giving the iPad back to stop the scream.** Articles warn it backfires; mothers do it to escape people's looks (Asharq Al-Awsat snippet).
- **Forcing or hiding food, diets, supplements** (prior run). Advisers say no forcing; mothers drift to tests and diets.
- **Waiting for a diagnosis** to unlock help (#14). This is the strongest "why now" for an assessment that doesn't need one.
- **Avoiding outings and visits altogether** (#10), which costs her family standing (#28).
- **Hitting or shouting back,** then guilt (#7).

## 5. Trust signals for a cold mother

**Builds trust:** naming *her exact moment* (the 10:30pm scream, the car seat, the iPad second); steps she can try tonight; "this is common, you're not doing it wrong"; tips that sound like they come from another mother (#20); honestly saying when to see a doctor or check hearing; a free assessment that **doesn't require a diagnosis** (#14).
**Breaks trust:** labels («التوحد», «عنيد», «مدلع»); cure or "within a week" promises (forum «شفاء» burnout); generic "10 tips" lists (webteb, mawdoo3 dominate search, and she has read them); any hint that she caused it (#7, the refrigerator-mother history); clinical jargon; recommending gear (ear defenders, supplements).
