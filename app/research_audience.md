### The real recurring moments (beat level)
- **Bedtime:** pyjamas → she lies down beside him → he keeps checking she's still there → she goes still in the dark, scrolling her phone → she wakes at 11 or 12 still in his bed, with the kitchen undone (#7, #8, #10). Then **he wakes at night and walks to her bed** (#5). She and her husband end up sleeping in separate rooms (#3, #4). His evening bedtime decides whether she gets any time with her husband (#7).
- **Chores:** he's awake → the vacuum, blender or hair dryer goes on → he covers his ears or cries and comes to cling to her → she switches it off → «بكنس لما ينام» → he sleeps → she's spent (#11, #12, #18). Cooking moves to nap time or night, and that causes friction at home (#11).
- **Shower:** she steps in → he cries or screams at the door, or bangs on it → she showers with the door open, or in two minutes, or only at night (#19–21).
- **Hair:** the haircut or hair wash is a scream every time → she puts it off (#22, #23).
- **Phone:** she picks up a call → he escalates → she cuts the call or hides in the bathroom (#25; English only).

### The silent thoughts and the ironic twists about her
Raw material in the winning shape. The Arabic lines are **seeds we wrote, not quotes**.
1. **Her bed is now his.** He arrives at 2 a.m. and she moves to the edge, or to his bed. Seed: «يجيك نص الليل، وتصحي الصبح وأنتي اللي في سريره؟»
2. **She sleeps apart from her husband because of bedtime** (#3, #4). Handle it gently: no marriage drama.
3. **Her evening is the time she loses.** She plans "after he sleeps" and then sleeps first (#7, #8).
4. **She whispers and creeps out of his room like a thief.** It's the classic sneak-out (a sleeplady.com expert warns against it [PARA]).
5. **Cooking moved to night** (#11). Seed: «صرتي تطبخي غدا بكرة وهو نايم؟»
6. **She showers on his schedule** (#19–21). Seed: «صرتي تتروشي بس لما ينام؟»
7. **The blender and dryer are banned while he's awake.** She dries her hair with a towel and makes juice at night. This is plausible, but **I found no quote** for it.
8. **She vacuums in "pieces"** (one room, then off), or sweeps by hand to avoid the noise. The broom-instead-of-vacuum detail is unverified.
9. **She cuts his hair while he sleeps.** This is a known parent hack, **unverified in Arabic**.
10. **She takes calls in the bathroom or the car** (#25, English only).
11. **"I'll do it when he sleeps" has become her whole to-do list** (#12, #13, #18). It's the umbrella twist.

### What she has tried and dropped, and why
- **Lying down and sneaking out** → he wakes, so she has to start over (sleeplady.com [PARA]).
- **An alarm so she doesn't fall asleep beside him** (#8) → it saves her evening but doesn't move bedtime forward. This is the same workaround Anhar rejected in Batch 1.
- **Moving the cot away from her bed** → it worked at first, then went back (3roos «هل ينام طفلك معك في الغرفة» [PARA]).
- **Letting him cry** → guilt and dread (#5 asks it as a question). Avoid it.
- **Chores in fixed blocks, waking early, a "laundry day"** → hawaaworld advice [PARA]. It's unrealistic for a tired mum.
- **Warning him before switching on the vacuum** («انا بشغل المكنسة هي صوتها عالي») → this is the most repeated advice (an X tweet from a professional, Lovevery, NHS). **She has heard it**, so it's generic.
- **Vacuuming with him on her hip, or putting him in a highchair in another room** (#16, Romper) → it needs two hands or a second room.
- **Ear muffs** → sold everywhere, so it's generic. Sources even say avoid them except when necessary.
- **A playpen by the shower door, or the TV on** (r/Mommit [PARA]) → the screaming continues.
- **Waiting for her husband to get home** before doing anything (a Reddit thread [PARA]).

### Trust builders and breakers
**Builders**
- Name what she reorganised, **without** judging it.
- Treat lying beside him as normal and loving, not a "bad habit". The popular Arabic TikTok framing is that co-sleeping isn't wrong; it becomes a problem when it drains her (@leen.aljord).
- Give tips that take seconds, with no prep, inside the chore itself.
- Name the goal she actually has: him sleeping on his own, her evening back.

**Breakers**
- Every Arabic article ends with «استشيري مختص/طبيب».
- MSA article tone and "make a routine" lists.
- Cry-it-out pressure.
- Safety lectures about co-sleeping.
- Blame such as «أنتي عودتيه».
- **Many results tie ear-covering to a diagnosis.** We must never echo that. Describe only what she sees.
