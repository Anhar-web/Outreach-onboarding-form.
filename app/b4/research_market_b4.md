# Market research, Batch 4 (step 3): emotions, outbursts, relatives, "it's not stubbornness"

*Method: marketing-sophistication (map, then stage, then gap, then match-check). This builds on `app/research_market.md`, which already found tantrums at Stage 5 and named the Arabic wallpaper («احتوي مشاعره», «سمّي مشاعره», «اصبري»). This file adds what's new for B4. **Evidence gap:** web search doesn't index TikTok or Instagram captions. The Arabic evidence comes from the articles those accounts recycle (Sayidaty, Asharq Al-Awsat, Sabq, Altibbi, Al Jazeera, Eremnews). The English evidence comes from the sources Arabic creators translate (Dr. Becky, Big Little Feelings, Mother.ly, Child Mind). I found no Saudi-dialect creator who clearly owns these topics. That matches the B1 finding, but it's still "observed", not proven.*

## 1. Sophistication stage, with evidence

| Topic | English market | Arabic market | Her heart |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tantrums and outbursts in public or at gatherings | **Stage 5** | **Stage 4** | Stage 5 |
| "It's not stubbornness / not bad behaviour, it's…" | **Stage 4–5** | **Stage 3** | Stage 5 |
| Relatives' unwanted advice | Stage 3 | **Stage 2** (articles only) | Stage 5 |
| Calming tips and emotion regulation | Stage 5 | Stage 4 | Stage 5 |

**Wallpaper (don't lead with any of these):**
- **"Be patient / stay calm first."** «حافظي على هدوئك» appears in every article (Okaz, Sabq, Maspero). «نظرات الناس لا تهم» is the actual Asharq Al-Awsat headline ("people's looks don't matter").
- **"Every child is different"** and "it's a normal stage." Altibbi calls stubbornness a normal bid for independence. (Our «طبيعي» ban already keeps us out of this.)
- **"He's not giving you a hard time, he's having a hard time."** This is Dr. Becky's line, repeated by Mother.ly, Psychology Today, Today's Parent and a dozen blogs. It's the master template of the "it's not X, it's Y" reframe.
- **"Not attention-seeking, connection-seeking."** NBC Think, Nurtured First, Evolutionary Parenting. Also wallpaper: "ignoring tantrums doesn't work" (Mother.ly).
- **"Tantrum vs meltdown."** This is the sensory explanation (Child Mind, Psychology Today, The Mighty, OT Toolbox). It's saturated in English, and it's **off-limits for us anyway** (it's clinical and implies a diagnosis).
- **"Name it to tame it" / "name the emotion."** Already contested inside the field: Dr. Becky says stop naming feelings mid-meltdown, and Hand in Hand says naming can make it worse. Contested wallpaper is the worst kind to lead with.
- **Calm-down corners and emotion charts.** The Bump, WebMD, Mother.ly and Generation Mindful all add the same caveat ("introduce it when calm, not as punishment").
- **"Connection before correction"** and "validate, then set limits" (the CDC's emotion coaching).
- **For relatives:** «اشكريها وتجاهلي النصيحة» ("thank her and ignore the advice") and «وحّدي القواعد بين البيتين» ("make the rules the same in both houses") (Al Jazeera, Sayidaty, e3arabi).

**Exhaustion signal (this is what makes Stage 5).** There is a backlash against gentle parenting: CBC and Romper's "gentle parenting dropouts", and Olivia Owen's 2.6M-view spoof asking her melting-down child for consent to a hug at IKEA. Eremnews has published «كيف جعل تيك توك الآباء يخشون قول لا» ("how TikTok made parents afraid to say no"). The audience is now cynical about *scripts* themselves. In the same feed, the Arabic-covered "Jessica" trick (calling an imaginary person to stop the crying) got 11M+ views. **People are tired of explanations and still hungry for tricks.**

## 2. Our batch's level vs the market's

- **The voice is Stage 5.** Witness scenes, her inner voice, a comic image of her, a love line that clears her. That's identity, and it's what the legacy breakouts did.
- **The tool is Stage 3–4.** One named, trick-like move shown working. Against an Arabic field of advice lists («حضّري مسبقاً، أعطيه خيارات، اهدئي»), that reads as a new mechanism.
- **The proof is Stage 3.** A personalised, evolving home plan built from her observations.
- **Fit: good, with one risk.** The «الافتراض» series uses the exact shape the English market has worn out ("it's not stubbornness, it's…"). In Arabic it's only Stage 3, so it still lands, *as long as* the second half isn't the borrowed one. If «لكن أحياناً…» resolves into sensory overload, "connection-seeking" or "having a hard time", the post joins the wallpaper *and* breaks our rules. **The fix:** the second half names an observable moment ("sometimes the hard part is the leaving, not the shoes"), never a cause inside him, and goes straight to the tool. The tool is the payoff, not the reframe.

## 3. The open lanes

**(a) The «الافتراض» series: the room's rules, not the child's psychology.**
Everyone debunks *labels for the child* (stubborn, manipulative, attention-seeking). **Nobody debunks the social rules of the gathering**: «لازم يسلّم» (he has to greet everyone), «كلمة وحدة مني ويسكت» (one word from me and he'll be quiet), «عطوه وبيسكت» (give it to him and he'll stop), «كان ملاك طول الزيارة» (he was an angel the whole visit). These are Saudi-specific, family-specific, and untranslated from English. How to state them without blaming her or sounding like "not like other advice":
- The label is always in **a relative's mouth, in quotes** («قالت خالته: …»). She's never the one who believes it.
- **Don't argue with the assumption.** Show what it costs *her* in the room (the comic image), then «لكن أحياناً…» plus one observable detail.
- **No meta-claims** («كل النصائح غلط», «عكس اللي يقولونه لك»). The series label does the contrast. Never knock other advice or the relatives themselves. They're family, and the humour stays on her.

**(b) Trick-like tools vs advice.** The Arabic field gives advice for *before* and *after* («حضّري», «بعد ما يهدأ كلميه»). The English field gives *home* tools (corners, charts, scripts). **The open lane is a tool that works in the room, in ten seconds, in front of witnesses**: no corner, no chart, no quiet bedroom. A legacy tool like the wrong shoe is exactly this, and the 11M Jessica trick proves the appetite. Constraint: our tools must be honest moves she makes, not distraction gimmicks promising instant silence. Show "what he does next" in 2–3 beats, never "and it stops".

**(c) Witness scenes with humour on the mother.** The market splits into earnest validation ("you're not a bad mom", "people's looks don't matter") and humour aimed at gentle parents (the IKEA spoof). **Nobody does affectionate humour about her as the referee of the whole majlis**: the aunt's commentary, grandma's counter-ruling, the uncle's «خلوه عندي يوم». The relatives' lines are the chorus. She gets the single comic image («المتحدثة الرسمية باسم الطفل», "the child's official spokeswoman"). The confession twist lands the laugh on her. This is the shape B3's winners (ملكة, جمعة, jacket) and legacy L01 (203K) share.

## 4. The mechanism to lead with (when the room is crowded)

Name it plainly. Don't lead with «خطة مخصصة» ("a customised plan"): sleep consultants and coaching ads have made that wallpaper. Lead with the two parts nobody else claims: **built from what she notices**, and **it changes with him**.

- **Proof-slide wording (safe):** «الخطة الحية: خطة للبيت تنبني من ملاحظاتك أنتي، موقف واحد في كل مرة، وتتعدل حسب استجابة طفلك.» ("The Living Plan: a home plan built from your own observations, one situation at a time, adjusted to how your child responds.")
- **Show it, don't advertise it.** The tool slide can include one *noticing* beat ("notice whether it starts at the door or at the shoes"). That *is* the method in miniature.
- **Don't claim:**
  - "every two weeks" (dropped in B2)
  - "looks at his whole day"
  - "finds the reason / why he does it"
  - "calmer in X days"
  - "proven"
  - testimonials
  - "specialists"
  - six areas
- **Brand "we" is allowed for the method** (Anhar kept «نبدأ بموقف واحد»). Parent "we" stays banned.
- **Odd posts** (on the `?focus=` route) must not say "assessment" on the proof or CTA slide.
- **Emotions CTA:** the outcome is "help him handle hard emotions and recover; calm before it becomes a scene". Name it with certainty, as the goal, not as a promised result.

## 5. Identity angle

**She is the one who knows which minute it turns, and the room doesn't.**
- Every relative in the scene has a theory built from five minutes of watching. She has years of watching.
- The identity line: **"you're the expert in the room, even when you're the only one who isn't talking."** («أنتي أكثر وحدة تعرفه… حتى لو كل المجلس عنده رأي.»)
- It answers stranger checklists and relatives' rulings without attacking either.
- It sets up the mechanism for free: a plan built *from her observations* is the logical next step for the person who already knows him best.
- **Avoid:** "trust your gut" mom-power slogans (English wallpaper), martyr framing, and any label on him.

## 6. Assumption candidates for «الافتراض اللي يصعّب الموضوع»

All of them belong to the room. None is hers. «عناد» (L01) is excluded as already used.

1. **"He does it in front of people on purpose, to embarrass you."**
   - Arabic: «قدام الناس يسويها… عشان يحرجك»
   - Whose: aunts and guests. Topic: anger and outbursts at a gathering.
   - Why open: English answers this with "connection-seeking" jargon, and Arabic doesn't answer it at all. Our answer is observable: "sometimes it's the crowd and the noise of the moment, not a plan." Phrase it without sensory vocabulary, and lead to a room tool.

2. **"Give him what he wants and he'll calm down."**
   - Arabic: «عطوه اللي يبي وبيسكت»
   - Whose: grandma or the uncle (the biscuit you just said no to). Topic: anger and outbursts, relatives.
   - Why open: articles talk about "unifying the rules". Nobody stages the moment the room overrules her mid-outburst, which is very funny on her («قاضية… والحكم انتقض بالاستئناف», "a judge whose ruling just got overturned on appeal").

3. **"One firm word from me and he'll stop."**
   - Arabic: «خلوه عندي يوم… كلمة وحدة مني ويسكت»
   - Whose: the uncle or the father-in-law. Topic: anger and outbursts.
   - Why open: the classic Gulf relative line, untouched online. Strong witness comedy (the uncle tries, the comic image lands on her watching). No blame, because the one who's wrong is the confident outsider.

4. **"He was an angel the whole visit, so it went fine."**
   - Arabic: «ما شاء الله كان ملاك طول الزيارة»
   - Whose: the hosts or the teacher. Topic: emotions (the explosion in the car or at home afterwards; a new tool for L12).
   - Why open: English calls this "restraint collapse", which is clinical jargon and banned for us. Arabic has nothing. Every mother recognises "the car ride home".

5. **"He has to greet everyone and kiss grandma's head."**
   - Arabic: «لازم يسلّم على الكل ويبوس راس جدته»
   - Whose: the family elders. Topic: outings and visits / communication.
   - Why open: purely Saudi and never touched. A refusal to greet reads as «قلة أدب» (rudeness), so it hits her. A tool like "a greeting he chooses" (a wave, a high five) honours the elders without the standoff at the door.

6. **"You've spoiled him."**
   - Arabic: «دلّعتيه… عشان كذا صاير كذا»
   - Whose: the mother-in-law or older relatives. Topic: anger and outbursts.
   - Why open: articles answer it with "explain calmly to grandma". The open move is the legacy love line: «انفجاره معك مو دليل إنك أقل… أحياناً دليل إنك أمانه». It clears her without saying the relative is wrong. **Handle with care:** the line itself blames her, so the post must clear her by slide 2.

7. **"Don't cry, that's shameful… men don't cry."**
   - Arabic: «عيب… الرجال ما يبكون»
   - Whose: relatives (often said kindly). Topic: emotions.
   - Why open: "name the emotion" is English wallpaper, but nobody stages the Gulf «عيب» moment and gives her a ten-second move to use *in front of* the speaker. **Avoid** turning it into a gender lecture.

8. **"He'll grow out of it."**
   - Arabic: «لما يكبر بيعقل… خليه»
   - Whose: everyone, kindly. Topic: any; best fits transitions or outbursts.
   - Why open: nobody argues with it because it's comforting. Our angle isn't "it's wrong". It's "waiting is a plan with no first step", which is a natural bridge to "one situation at a time".
   - **No promise** that acting now changes anything.

**Recommended four (emotions lead):** #3 «كلمة وحدة مني», #4 «كان ملاك», #2 «عطوه وبيسكت», #5 «لازم يسلّم». Keep #1 and #6 as one-tool alternates.
