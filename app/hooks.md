## Hook Specialist

These are 18 hooks, all in the Batch 1 winning shape: her exact moment, then a true, gentle twist about the workaround she has built around him. None of them opens on his behaviour as a problem to fix. Every hook works as a one-liner (the form the two winners used). Where a second half adds a concrete promise, it is given as option (b).
Scores use the four Hook Point drivers: PS = perspective shift, Del = delivery, Punch = hook punch, Ten = self-reflection/tension. Each is out of 5, plus an overall filter score out of 5. They are a sorting aid only. Anhar's read-aloud judgment is the real test (Addendum 2, §7.9).
Where a word or detail is unverified (no Gulf quote in the research) or might sound translated, I say so.

### Angle A: "The workarounds" (Lane A, the main lane)
**The lane:** name the life she has quietly rearranged around him, in the exact grammar of the winners: *[his moment], فصرتي [her workaround]?*
**Why now:** this is the proven shape (4.4K / 3.9K), and the research shows no competitor names it. In Arabic, the topic is close to Stage 1 when framed around *her* housework, rest and hygiene. The steps then undo that one workaround a little at a time.

**A1. Shower**
- (a) «يدق على باب الحمام أول ما تسكريه، فصرتي تتروشي والباب مفتوح؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها قبل ما تدخلي، وكل وحدة تاخذ ثواني.»
- Gloss: "He bangs on the bathroom door as soon as you close it, so now you shower with the door open?" / "Here are three things you do before you go in, and each takes seconds."
- Mechanism: recognition + ironic twist (her privacy has quietly gone).
- Topic: shower on his schedule.
- Value: three seconds-long, no-prep things before she steps in (the same promise as the vacuum post, which won).
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 5 · Ten 4 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. The English sources say "screams at the door". Banging on the door is the same moment told in the Saudi way.

**A2. Cooking at night**
- (a) «أول ما تدخلي المطبخ يتعلق فيكي، فصرتي تطبخي غدا بكرة وهو نايم؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها وأنتي واقفة عند القدر، وما تحتاج أي تجهيز.»
- Gloss: "The moment you walk into the kitchen he clings to you, so now you cook tomorrow's lunch while he's asleep?" / "Three things you do while standing at the pot, no prep needed."
- Mechanism: ironic twist (lunch made at night), close to the vacuum line's shape.
- Topic: chores sequel (cooking at night).
- Value: things she can do during the cooking itself, not a reorganised day.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 5 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. «غدا بكرة» is everyday Saudi speech. A Gulf snippet backs the cooking-at-night detail (3roos #11).

**A3. Hair dryer**
- (a) «يغطي أذنه أول ما تشغلي السشوار، فصرتي تنشفي شعرك بالفوطة؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها والسشوار في يدك.»
- Gloss: "He covers his ears the moment you turn on the hair dryer, so now you dry your hair with a towel?" / "Three things you do with the dryer in your hand."
- Mechanism: ironic twist, and a deliberate sequel echo of the vacuum winner.
- Topic: noise sequel (hair dryer).
- Value: seconds-long tips with the dryer in her hand, the same promise that worked for the vacuum.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risks:** (1) it is very close in structure to the vacuum hook, which helps as a sequel but could feel like a copy. (2) There is no quote from a mother who gave up her dryer (research gap). (3) «يغطي أذنه» is the line search results tie to a diagnosis. We only describe it, but don't stack it with any other ear or noise wording on the slides.

**A4. Night waking / her bed**
- (a) «يجي سريرك كل ليلة الساعة ثنتين، فصرتي تنامي على طرف السرير؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث خطوات لما يجيك نص الليل، ترجعيه فيها لسريره بهدوء.»
- Gloss: "He comes to your bed every night at two, so now you sleep on the edge of the bed?" / "Three steps for when he comes in at night, to take him back to his bed calmly."
- Mechanism: recognition + ironic twist (her own bed is now his).
- Topic: bedtime sequel (night waking).
- Value: what to do at 2 a.m., in steps. It names the goal (him back in his bed) and describes what *she* does, without promising a result.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 5 · Ten 5 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. Several Gulf titles back it (#3–#6).

**A5. Phone calls**
- (a) «أول ما تردي على الجوال يجي يشد يدك ويعلي صوته، فصرتي تكلمي أمك من الحمام؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها قبل ما تردي، في نفس الثواني اللي الجوال يرن فيها.»
- Gloss: "As soon as you answer the phone he pulls your hand and gets louder, so now you talk to your mum from the bathroom?" / "Three things you do in the seconds while it's ringing."
- Mechanism: ironic twist. "Your mum" makes it Saudi and warm.
- Topic: phone calls.
- Value: tips that fit the ring window (seconds, no prep).
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 4 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: mostly passes. **Risk:** the first half is long (two actions). The evidence is English only. «من الحمام» might need to be «من دورة المياه» in her area (spelling question for Anhar).

**A6. Haircut**
- (a) «كل قصة شعر صياح من أولها لآخرها، فصرتي تقصي له وهو نايم؟»
- Gloss: "Every haircut is screaming from start to finish, so now you cut his hair while he sleeps?"
- Mechanism: ironic twist.
- Topic: haircut.
- Value: one-liner. The post would give steps for the haircut while he's awake.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 4 · Punch 4 · Ten 3 → **3**
- Read-aloud: passes as speech. **Risks:** the cutting-while-asleep hack is unverified in Arabic, and scissors near a sleeping child may draw safety comments. It is also less universal than A1–A4. If we run a haircut post, B6 is the safer version.

### Angle B: "It starts before the moment" (Lane B, the bridge lane)
**The lane:** her moment + the twist about her, then the turn: the hard part begins earlier than the moment she's fighting.
**Why now:** this is our real mechanism (his whole day, not the moment alone), and it is Anhar's own bedtime CTA («لاحظي ايش يصير خلال اليوم قبل وقت النوم»). So the hook sets up the CTA without a jump. It is a Stage-4 perspective shift on topics (bedtime) where she already knows the chair method. Wording stays "starts before", never "X causes Y".

**B1. Sneaking out**
- (a)+(b) «تقضي ساعة تنيميه، وأول ما تقومي من جمبه يفتح عيونه؟ الطلعة من غرفته تبدأ قبل ما تنسدحي.»
- Gloss: "You spend an hour getting him to sleep, and the moment you get up from beside him his eyes open? Getting out of his room starts before you lie down."
- Mechanism: recognition + perspective shift + open loop.
- Topic: bedtime sequel (sneaking out).
- Value: the steps for getting out happen *before* she lies down: new, not a repeat of the chair step-back.
- Scores: PS 5 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 5 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. Both halves are one thought in one breath. **Check:** the twist is mostly in the second half, so (a) alone is plain recognition. Always run it with (b).

**B2. Her evening lost**
- (a)+(b) «تنيميه الساعة ثمان، وتطلعي من غرفته الساعة عشر؟ الساعتين هذي تبدأ من العصر، وفيها ثلاث أشياء تقدري تغيريها من بكرة.»
- Gloss: "You put him to bed at eight and come out of his room at ten? Those two hours start in the afternoon, and there are three things in them you can change from tomorrow."
- Mechanism: time-math recognition (her evening, gone) + perspective shift.
- Topic: bedtime sequel (her evening lost).
- Value: three changes in his afternoon, starting tomorrow (the same promise shape as the approved Batch 1 bedtime hook, moved to a new hour).
- Scores: PS 5 · Del 4 · Punch 5 · Ten 5 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risk:** «ثلاث أشياء تقدري تغيريها» echoes Batch 1. Fine for a sequel, but the copywriter must not reuse that post's steps. "Start in the afternoon" has to stay a noticing point, not a cause.

**B3. Night waking, the half-asleep decision**
- (a)+(b) «يجي سريرك الساعة ثنتين، وأنتي تقرري وأنتي نص نايمة ايش تسوي؟ هذا القرار ينحسم من وقت النوم، مو الساعة ثنتين.»
- Gloss: "He comes to your bed at two, and you're deciding what to do while half asleep? That decision gets settled at bedtime, not at two."
- Mechanism: ironic twist (making big calls half-asleep) + perspective shift.
- Topic: bedtime sequel (night waking).
- Value: a decided plan for 2 a.m., set up at bedtime.
- Scores: PS 5 · Del 4 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: **risk.** «ينحسم» may sound a little written. «تقرريه من وقت النوم» is the plainer alternative. It also overlaps A4's moment, so don't run both.

**B4. Shower**
- (a)+(b) «تدخلي تتروشي دقيقتين، وتطلعي تلاقيه يبكي ورا الباب؟ الشاور يبدأ قبل ما تسكري الباب.»
- Gloss: "You go in for a two-minute shower and come out to find him crying behind the door? The shower starts before you close the door."
- Mechanism: recognition + perspective shift.
- Topic: shower.
- Value: what to set up in the minute before she goes in.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. A1 has the stronger twist. Use this one if the shower post is F5 or F1.

**B5. Blender**
- (a) «صرتي تطفي الخلاط أول ما تسمعي خطواته جاية للمطبخ؟»
- (b) + «اللي يفرق يصير قبل ما تشغليه، وهذي ثلاث أشياء تاخذ ثواني.»
- Gloss: "You've started switching off the blender the moment you hear his footsteps coming to the kitchen?" / "What makes the difference happens before you switch it on, and here are three things that take seconds."
- Mechanism: ironic twist (she reacts before he does) + perspective shift.
- Topic: noise sequel (blender).
- Value: seconds-long things before she switches it on.
- Scores: PS 5 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: (a) passes and is vivid. **Risk:** "before you switch it on" pulls toward the generic tip "warn him first" (research: she's heard it). The copywriter must not use that as one of the three things.

**B6. Haircut**
- (a)+(b) «تأجلي قصة شعره أسبوع ورا أسبوع، لأنك عارفة ايش ينتظرك عند الحلاق؟ القصة تبدأ في البيت، قبل ما تطلعوا.»
- Gloss: "You put off his haircut week after week because you know what's waiting for you at the barber's? The haircut starts at home, before you leave."
- Mechanism: recognition (her postponing) + perspective shift.
- Topic: haircut.
- Value: the home steps before the barber.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 4 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. A Gulf title backs it (#22).

### Angle C: "The quiet trade" (what the workaround costs her)
**The lane:** the market's third direction, "the true cost of that workaround to her", said warmly and never as martyrdom. The irony shows what she has given up (her bed, her mum's call, her own evening), then hands her the first step back. Two of these use her inner voice in quotes, which produced the emotional peaks in Batch 1 (Test Panel, B8).
**Why I didn't pick named-enemy:** Addendum 2 records that the «قالوا لك…» angle against ready-made advice was rejected. A cost angle keeps her at the centre instead of us arguing with other advice.

**C1. "Just tonight"**
- (a) «تقولي "الليلة بس" وتخليه ينام جمبك، وصارت كل ليلة؟»
- (b) + «مو غلط إنه ينام جمبك. بس إذا تبغي سريرك يرجع لك، هذي أول خطوة.»
- Gloss: "You say 'just tonight' and let him sleep beside you, and it's become every night?" / "It's not wrong that he sleeps beside you. But if you want your bed back, here's the first step."
- Mechanism: inner-voice quote + ironic twist + naming her fear (A3-approved move).
- Topic: bedtime sequel (comes to her bed).
- Value: the first step, with permission (no blame, no sleep-safety lecture).
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 5 · Ten 5 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. This is how she'd tell her sister. Spoken words in straight quotes, as A5 requires.

**C2. Separate rooms**
- (a) «تنامي في غرفته كل ليلة، وزوجك صار ينام لحاله في غرفتكم؟»
- Gloss: "You sleep in his room every night, and your husband has started sleeping alone in your room?"
- Mechanism: recognition of the cost.
- Topic: bedtime sequel.
- Value: one-liner. The post gives the step-out steps.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 5 · Ten 5 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risk:** marriage is sensitive on Saudi TikTok. Comments may turn to the husband. It's real (Gulf titles #3, #4), but it needs Anhar's call. Hold as reserve.

**C3. Hair wash**
- (a) «تغسلي شعره وهو يصيح، وتطلعي من الحمام وأنتي اللي مبلولة أكثر منه؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها وأنتي ماسكة الشاور، وكل وحدة تاخذ ثواني.»
- Gloss: "You wash his hair while he screams, and come out of the bathroom wetter than him?" / "Three things you do with the shower head in your hand, each one seconds."
- Mechanism: ironic twist about her, in the «وأنتي اللي…» grammar of the bedtime winner.
- Topic: hair wash.
- Value: seconds-long tips during the wash.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 5 · Ten 4 → **5**
- Read-aloud: passes. The twist is visual and true, and it's a smile *with* her. It isn't a joke that needs explaining. Backed by Mumsnet #23 (English).

**C4. The "after he sleeps" list**
- (a) «الكنس بعد ما ينام، والطبخ بعد ما ينام، والشاور بعد ما ينام، ولما ينام تنامي قبل ما تبدئي؟»
- (b) + «ابدئي بوحدة بس، وهذي ثلاث خطوات ترجعها للنهار.»
- Gloss: "Vacuuming after he sleeps, cooking after he sleeps, showering after he sleeps, and when he sleeps you fall asleep before you start?" / "Start with just one, and here are three steps to bring it back into the day."
- Mechanism: the umbrella twist, as a rhythmic triple build (tension) with an ironic payoff.
- Topic: chores sequel (the "after he sleeps" to-do list).
- Value: pick one chore and three steps to move it back into the day (one area at a time, our method in miniature).
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 4 · Punch 4 · Ten 5 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risks:** it is the longest hook, so on the slide each «بعد ما ينام» needs its own line. «الكنس» calls back to the vacuum post, which is good for a sequel. «ابدئي» is an instruction, but it's offered as a starting point, like the approved «جهزي اللي بعده».

**C5. Calling her mum**
- (a) «تبغي تكلمي أمك، فتستني إلين ما ينام عشان تقدري تسمعيها؟»
- (b) + «هذي ثلاث أشياء تسويها قبل ما تتصلي، عشان تكلميها وهو صاحي.»
- Gloss: "You want to call your mum, so you wait until he's asleep so you can actually hear her?" / "Three things you do before you dial, so you can talk to her while he's awake."
- Mechanism: the cost (her own mother's voice) + ironic twist.
- Topic: phone calls.
- Value: before-the-call steps. An outgoing call, so prep is realistic.
- Scores: PS 4 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 4 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risk:** «عشان تكلميها وهو صاحي» leans toward a promise. If the copywriter can't deliver it honestly, drop (b).

**C6. Sneaking out, beat by beat**
- (a) «تطلعي من غرفته على أطراف أصابعك، وتسكري الباب بشويش، وتوقفي وراه تتسمعي إذا صحى؟»
- Gloss: "You tiptoe out of his room, close the door slowly, and stand behind it listening in case he woke up?"
- Mechanism: a beat-by-beat scene (A4 pattern 6). The tension builds over three beats, and the cost (her held breath in her own home) is felt rather than stated.
- Topic: bedtime sequel (sneaking out).
- Value: one-liner. The post gives the steps for getting out.
- Scores: PS 3 · Del 5 · Punch 4 · Ten 5 → **4**
- Read-aloud: passes. **Risk:** «على أطراف أصابعك» may be the written form. Her area might say «على رؤوس أصابعك». Ask in the spelling batch. It's weaker on perspective shift than B1, which covers the same moment.

### Recommended hook per angle
- **Angle A: A4** (2 a.m., the edge of the bed). It's the purest copy of the winning grammar, on the strongest-evidenced sequel, and it's universal for any toddler mother.
- **Angle B: B1** (an hour to get him to sleep, and his eyes open as she gets up). This is the clearest true perspective shift, and it sets up the "before the moment" CTA without a jump.
- **Angle C: C1** («"الليلة بس"… وصارت كل ليلة؟»). Her inner voice plus the twist, with a no-blame second half that names her real goal.

### Hooks I cut and why
- «صرتي تحفظي الباب من أي زاوية يزيّق، عشان تطلعي وهو نايم؟» It takes a second to decode, and I'm unsure of the verb in her dialect. Cut as likely translated.
- «صار عندك قائمة "بعد ما ينام" أطول من يومك كله؟» Forced hyperbole that needs a beat to land. C4 says the same thing plainly.
- «قالوا لك "قولي له قبل لا تشغلي المكنسة"؟ …» A named-enemy angle against advice. The «قالوا لك» angle was already rejected (Addendum 2, round 2), and it puts us at the centre instead of her.
- «تطبخي بيد وحدة وهو على جنبك يبغى يشوف القدر؟» It would invite stove-safety lectures in the comments. That's a trust breaker.
- «صرتي تسوي العصير بالليل عشان صوت الخلاط؟» Lukewarm ("meh"). B5's footsteps detail is the live version.
- «يجيك نص الليل، وتصحي الصبح وأنتي اللي في سريره؟» (the research seed). It copies «وأنتي اللي نمتي» too closely, and the scene logic needs explaining (how did she end up there?). Replaced by A4.
- «يصحى الساعة ثنتين ويجي سريرك؟ اللي يرجعه لسريره يبدأ الساعة ثمان.» It implies a cause ("what returns him") and promises a result for the child. Rebuilt as B3.
- «تستني إلين ما يرجع أبوه عشان تتروشي؟» True (a Reddit pattern), but it shifts the focus to the husband's absence. A1 does the shower better.
- «تنيميه الساعة ثمان… وفي الآخر تكتشفي إنك ما تغديتي؟» The meals moment has zero quote evidence (research gaps), and it tips into martyrdom.
- «قهوتك تبرد كل يوم…» A generic mum meme with no link to his hard moments or to a usable step.
