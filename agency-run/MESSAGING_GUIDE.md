# مدار البيت | الخطة الحية
## Messaging Guide: audience, market and positioning, in one place

Use this for anything written to mothers: emails, WhatsApp messages, social posts, captions, ads and future pages. It brings together the research, market analysis, positioning, brand belief, offer and voice work behind the Living Plan landing page, **updated with every decision you made afterwards**.

Where an earlier working file disagrees with this guide, this guide wins.

## 1. The non-negotiables (read first)

1. **Every message is about her, never about Anhar.** The founder never appears in a headline, subject line, hook or opening. First person («أنا») is used only to explain how the service works, for example «تجاوبي على أسئلة، وبعدها توصلك خطتك». Even there, prefer what SHE does and receives. Never write «أنا معاكي», «مع أنهار» or «أنا أنهار». They make the message about the founder and read like "misery loves company".
2. **Never mention therapy, speech sessions or specialists.** Never position the service as working "alongside" them. If a mother has sessions, she can say so privately and the plan takes it into account.
3. **Never assume a diagnosis.** Some children are undiagnosed or too young to be diagnosed. Don't write «أمهات الأطفال التوحديين» or «طفلك المصاب بالتوحد» in public copy. Talk about the repeating situations instead.
4. **Never promise a result for the child.** Don't promise that he sleeps, talks, eats more or calms down. Promise what the service delivers to HER: clarity, a next step, a plan built from his day, a review every two weeks, not starting from zero.
5. **Never blame the mother or label the child.** No «عنيد», «عدواني», «مدلع» or «عصبي». Describe what he does.
6. **Never use cure language.** No «علاج», «شفاء» or «يتخلص من».
7. **Never be lukewarm.** Every line should make her feel something or see her own day. Polite and safe means it gets ignored.
8. **Write natural Saudi Arabic, never translated.** Follow the language file.

## 3. Who she is

**Saudi mothers who keep facing the same difficult situations with their child.** Many children are autistic, some are undiagnosed, some are too young to be diagnosed. She arrives mostly from TikTok, on her phone, often with a child calling her and a full day around her.

**Her emotional state.** She is exhausted, isolated, alert to blame, and tired of promises.
- She carries the daily plan alone. Specialists see her child briefly, and home is where everything actually happens.
- She has felt judged by relatives and strangers.
- She is very sensitive to any hint that it's her fault.
- She has tried many things that worked for a while and then stopped working.

**What she already has:**
- Phones full of saved tips and screenshots.
- Advice from relatives.
- Articles listing "10 tips".
- Sometimes forum "programs", diets, or sessions.

**What she doesn't have:**
- One clear priority.
- Someone who knows her child's history and doesn't disappear between appointments.
- A plan that changes when her child changes.

**Her beliefs:**
- What she sees every day matters, but nobody asks her about it.
- What worked for another child didn't work for hers.
- She has tried enough. She is not lazy and not the problem.
- She is wary of paying for "more of the same".

**What she wants her day to look like.** Not perfect. A bedtime that ends. A family visit she doesn't dread. Knowing what to do tomorrow. She measures change in specific moments ("he woke up angry once this week instead of every day"), not in developmental milestones.

**The feeling every message should leave:**
> "Someone finally looked at my child's whole day with me, and treated me as the one who knows him."

That means respect, not pity. Partnership, not an expert talking down. Relief from clarity, not hype. Hope, without a promise.

## 4. Market sophistication

**Market level: 3 (mechanism), with a Stage 5 emotional heart.**

**What she has already heard (and stopped believing):**
- **Cure and program promises.** Forum threads about diets and programs that "heal", with early gains that fade. The pattern is: try a promised fix, see a partial or temporary change, then go back to searching.
- **Sessions that don't carry over to home.** The clearest real quote from the research: "My daughter was getting PT/OT/Speech, but who was there with an everyday plan to put it together?" (English, r/Autism_Parenting).
- **Generic advice everywhere.** "١٠ نصائح" lists on webteb, mawdoo3, sayidaty and Instagram.

**What this means for every message:**
- Opening with a bigger promise sounds like one more «شفاء» thread.
- Lead with **her own repeating moment, in her words**. Then turn it into the outcome she wants to live, and make it believable with the **how**: his whole day, one challenge at a time, reviewed every two weeks.
- Her emotional state is closer to Stage 5 (exhausted, judged, tired). So **open by recognising her day**, without blame, before anything else.

**The open lane.** Nobody visible in Arabic offers an ongoing, personal home plan, built from the child's whole day, with a fixed review every two weeks, in Saudi dialect. Everyone else sells a format: a session, a centre, a diet, or a tips list.

| What she can buy | Price signal | What's missing |
|---|---|---|
| Online freelance sessions (Khamsat) | $5–20 per session | One-off, no follow-up |
| Home sessions | ~150 ريال per session | Works with the child, not her day |
| Private centres | 100,000+ ريال a year (older press figure) | Expensive; skills stay at the centre |
| Charities, free caregiver training | Free | Generic, not continuous |
| Tips accounts, forum diets and "programs" | Free | More tips, "cure" promises |
| **الخطة الحية** | **٧٤٩ ريال / month** | **Ongoing, personal, built from her day, reviewed every two weeks** |

Don't compare the price to session costs on the page, and don't break it into a per-day price. The table is for your own understanding.

**Lead with:**
- Her scene, then the outcome she wants to live, then the how.

**Never lead with:**
- Outcome promises for the child.
- Cure words.
- "More tips", «أسرار», «دليل شامل».
- «الأول من نوعه» or «أفضل برنامج».
- «خطة مخصصة لطفلك» on its own.
- «طريق واضح تمشي عليه» on its own.
- "Understand why…" as the outcome. It reads like a PDF.
- Fear, or "before it's too late".

## 5. Positioning

**Core message (locked):**
> أنتي مو محتاجة تجمعي نصائح أكثر. تحتاجي طريقة واضحة تناسب طفلك وحياتكم.

**One-breath version:**
> أنتي مو محتاجة تجمعي نصائح أكثر. تحتاجي خطة تبدأ من يوم طفلك، وتتعدل معاكي كل أسبوعين.

**The mechanism: whole day, one challenge, every two weeks.**
> يومه كامل، تحدي واحد، ومراجعة كل أسبوعين.

**Why it's called "living":**
> اسمها الخطة الحية لأنها ما تنكتب مرة وتنتهي. تتغير مع طفلك.

**How it compares with what she's tried:**

| She's tried… | …which gave her | الخطة الحية gives her |
|---|---|---|
| Tips and articles | More information | One priority and the next step |
| Programs and diets | A fixed plan that promises a result | A plan that changes with her child, and no false promise |
| Trying alone | Starting from zero every time | Someone who knows her child's history, a review every two weeks |

**Differentiators:**
1. **It starts from his whole day, not only the behaviour.** Bedtime is the end of his day, and anger has things that build up before it. Case 1 is the proof.
2. **One challenge at a time, not ten tips a week.**
3. **Reviewed and adjusted every two weeks.** Hard days, setbacks and routine changes are part of the plan.
4. **Built from what she sees.** Her observations are the most important input.
5. **Low risk.** Monthly, and the plan adjusts if a step doesn't suit him.

**Who it's for:** a mother who wants a plan that starts from her child's day, wants to work on one thing clearly, and wants the plan to change with her as things change.

**Who it's not for (approved line):**
> تدوري حل سريع، أو نتيجة مضمونة. الخطة الحية تحتاج منك تطبقي وتلاحظي، وتتعدل معاكي خطوة بخطوة.

## 6. Brand beliefs (use as angles)

1. **What she sees every day is the most important information about her child.**
   «اللي تشوفيه كل يوم مو تفاصيل جانبية. هو أهم معلومة عن طفلك.»
2. **She sees details nobody else sees.**
   «أنتي اللي تشوفي يوم طفلك كامل، وتشوفي تفاصيل ما يشوفها أحد غيرك.»
3. **We don't try to change him. We understand his day with her.**
   «ما نحاول نغيّر طفلك. نحاول نفهم يومه معاكي، ونختار أول شي نشتغل عليه.»
4. **Most advice is written in general, not for every child.**
   «أغلب النصائح مكتوبة بشكل عام، مو لكل طفل. تجيك لموقف واحد، وما يرجع أحد يسألك ايش صار بعدها.»
5. **The problem was never her effort.**
   «المشكلة مو إنك ما حاولتي كفاية.»
   Use this as reassurance, and pair it with what she needs instead.
6. **One priority beats ten tips.** See the core message.
7. **A plan that never changes stops fitting him.** Hard days are part of the plan, «مو دليل إنها ما نفعت».
8. **Partnership.** She knows her child, the plan brings structure, and the next step is decided with her.

The only thing the brand stands against is generic, one-size advice standing in for understanding. Say that calmly. There is no enemy: not specialists, not relatives, not other mothers.

## 7. Her situations: scenes, words and angles

For each situation: the scene, what she's tried, the angle to lead with, what to avoid, and the approved headline. The headlines follow the approved pattern: **her challenge as a short question, then stacked outcomes she wants to live.**

### A. Bedtime (وقت النوم)
- **Scene:** a long time to fall asleep, night waking, very early waking, and her own exhaustion.
  «كل ليلة نفس القصة: البيجاما، والسرير، وبعدها ساعة وأكثر وأنتي تحاولي.»
- **What she's tried:** routines from videos and other accounts, which work for a few nights and then stop.
- **Angle:** bedtime is the end of his whole day. Her tiredness can be named too.
- **Avoid:** promising hours of sleep or "sleeps through the night".
- **Headline:** «كل ليلة نفس المعركة؟ خلي وقت النوم أهدى، وروتينه أوضح، ولياليكم أخف.»
- **Invite line:** «تخيلي يجي وقت النوم وأنتي عارفة خطوتك، بدل ما تجربي شي جديد كل ليلة.»

### B. Outings and family visits (العزايم، بيت الجدة، الزيارات)
- **Scene:** thinking about it the day before. Following him from room to room. Missing the conversation. Coming home exhausted, as if she never saw her family. Stares and comments. Turning down invitations.
- **Emotion:** the heaviest emotional load. The market is the least crowded here. Lead with her feelings, and keep it short.
- **Avoid:** repeating «مدلع / مو متربي», even as a quote.
- **Headline:** «كل عزيمة صارت همّ؟ روحي وأنتي مرتاحة، واستمتعي بوقتك مع أهلك.»
- **Problem lines:** «قبل العزيمة بيوم وأنتي تفكري: كيف بيكون؟ ومتى نرجع؟ وأول ما تدخلوا، تقضي الوقت تلحقيه من غرفة لغرفة، والسوالف تفوتك.»

### C. Communication (التواصل)
- **Scene:** he takes her hand to the kitchen and she guesses: water? food? something else? When she can't get it, he cries more and she gets more tense.
- **Angle:** the communication between them, his requests, a calmer day.
- **Avoid:** promising words or speech. Don't mention speech therapy.
- **Avoid this phrase too:** «خليه يوصل لك اللي يحتاجه». You flagged it as translated.
- **Headline:** «تخمّني طول اليوم ايش يبغى؟ خلي التواصل بينكم أسهل، وطلباته أوضح، ويومكم أهدى.»

### D. Food (الأكل)
- **Scene:** the same few foods every day. «وأي أكل جديد تحطيه في صحنه يرفضه.» She tries convincing him, hiding new food among what he likes, and mealtime itself has become a worry.
- **Angle:** the opposite of a diet. Questions first, his familiar foods stay, new foods come in gradually.
- **Avoid:** promising a longer food list or weight change. No nutrition claims. For health worries, «خذي رأي الطبيب».
- **Headline:** «نفس الثلاث أكلات كل يوم؟ خلي الأكل الجديد يدخل صحنه بالتدريج، وبدون معارك.»

### E. Transitions and turning off the iPad (الانتقال)
- **Scene:** «تقولي له خلاص نطفي الآيباد، وأنتي عارفة ايش بيصير بعدها.» She has tried five-minute warnings and counting, and it always ends the same way. Now she postpones turning it off to avoid what comes next.
- **Angle:** which transitions are hard for him, and what comes before them, instead of one warning for every child.
- **Headline:** «كل ما تطفي الآيباد تبدأ معركة؟ خلي الانتقال أوضح له، وأسهل عليكم.»

### F. Anger and outbursts (الغضب والانفعال)
- **Scene:** «يصحى متضايق، ويرمي الأغراض، وما يهدأ بسهولة. وأنتي طول اليوم تحاولي تتوقعي ايش بيضايقه بعدها.»
- **Angle:** this is where the proof is strongest. Use his whole day, not only the outburst. The case 1 quote sits nearby, labelled as one mother's experience.
- **Avoid:** if he hurts himself, never imply the plan handles it alone. Never use «عصبي» or «عدواني».
- **Headline:** «طول اليوم وأنتي على أعصابك؟ خلي يومكم أهدى، وتعاملي مع انفعاله وأنتي عارفة ايش تسوي.»

### General / other situations
- **Headline:** «نفس المواقف تتكرر كل يوم؟ وقفي الحلول المتفرقة، وابدئي بخطة تناسب طفلك.»
- **Problem lines:** «تجربي نصيحة من مقطع، وطريقة من حساب ثاني، وبعد كم يوم ترجعي لنفس النقطة. وكل مرة تبدأي من الصفر.»

### Approved subheadlines

**Standard:**
> خطة شخصية مبنية على يوم طفلك كامل، تركز على تحدي واحد، وتتعدل كل أسبوعين حسب استجابته، وما تضيعي شهور تجربي نصائح متفرقة لحالك.

**By her weakest area:**

| Weakest area | Subheadline |
|---|---|
| Patterns | خطة شخصية مبنية على يوم طفلك كامل، تشوفي فيها ايش يتكرر قبل الموقف، وتتعدل كل أسبوعين حسب استجابته، وتكوني مستعدة للموقف قبل ما يجي، بدل ما يفاجئك كل مرة. |
| Personalizing | خطة شخصية مبنية على طفلك أنتي، مو على نصيحة تنطبق على كل الأطفال، وتتعدل كل أسبوعين حسب استجابته، وما تضيعي شهور تجربي نصائح متفرقة. |
| Consistency | خطة شخصية تركز على تحدي واحد، بخطوات واضحة تمشي عليها، وتتعدل كل أسبوعين حسب استجابته، وما تبدأي من الصفر كل مرة. |

## 8. Proof: the three real cases

Use these exactly as supplied. Add nothing: no names, ages or extra details. Label them as mothers' experiences, not guaranteed results. Tell them about the mother and child, not about Anhar.

1. **He wakes up angry every day.** A mother reached out because her son was always angry, said unkind things, and didn't calm down easily. The questions covered his whole day, not just the behaviour: his routine, what happens around him, the situations he goes through. Things in his day that upset him and put pressure on him turned out to be an important part of the picture. Two weeks into the plan, the mother said:
   > «كان يصحى معصب كل يوم. هالأسبوع صحى معصب مرة وحدة.»

   This is the strongest proof and the only real Saudi mother quote available.
2. **Communication.** A mother saw a post and reached out. Communication was the area that needed support, so the plan focused on it. After two weeks, she said new behaviours had started to appear.
3. **Food.** The list of foods he accepted was short. Before any step, there were extra questions so no other need was missed. New foods were then introduced gradually, while his accepted foods stayed.

**Don't say «الحساسية».** Readers take it as allergy, and it implies a cause. Say «أشياء في يومه تضايقه وتضغط عليه».

## 10. Voice: the short version

The full rules are in the language file and Addendum 2.

**Words to use:**

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| ايش | وش |
| فين | وين |
| شي | شيء |
| أنتي / وأنتي | أنتِ |
| معاكي | معاكِ |
| دحين | الحين |
| ما في | ما فيه |
| قبل ما | قبل لا |
| بالتدريج | تدريجيًا |

- **Feminine verbs:** no extra ن. Write تحسي، تقدري، تعرفي، تبدأي.
- **Never use:** يمكن، ممكن، قد، ربما, in-between filler like بالضبط, em dashes, ellipses in headlines, or tanween-fath endings (اً).

**Structures that work:**
- Her challenge as a short question she says yes to, then outcomes she wants to live, stacked as commands (خلي… و… و…).
- Then the how: his whole day, one challenge, every two weeks.
- End by removing the pain: «وما تضيعي شهور تجربي نصائح متفرقة لحالك».

**Structures that failed:**
- Two half-sentences stitched together, as if by two writers.
- «افهمي ليش…» as the outcome.
- «أنا معاكي».
- A line that says what something is NOT before saying what it is («مو اشتراك في نصائح»).
- A sentence starting with «بدون ما…» after a full stop.
- Literal translations, such as «تفاعلية», «مو فشل لها», «وما أحد شافه», «يرجع مثل ما هو».

**Process before you send any copy:**
1. Write one thought per line.
2. Keep her as the subject.
3. Do the read-aloud test.
4. Write a literal English back-translation under every key line.
5. Check she can understand it in five seconds.
6. Check she'd feel something.
7. Don't self-score.

## 11. Channel notes

### Email
- **Tone:** a personal note from a knowledgeable person. No formal openings and no exclamation marks.
- **Subject lines:** use her scene, for example «كل ليلة نفس المعركة؟» or «قبل العزيمة بيوم…». Never use the founder's name as the hook.
- **Body:** one idea per email. Her moment, then a reframe (one belief from section 6), then one small, practical observation she can try, then one link or action.
- **Results follow-up:** reflect her situation, her weakest area and her Q15 words back to her. Give one small step she can try before any mention of the Living Plan. The assessment value must stand on its own.
- **Waitlist news:** the opening date and time, the first-come rule, and capacity with its real reason. No countdown pressure.

### WhatsApp waitlist group
- It's announcement-only. Anhar is the only one who posts.
- Keep messages short. Weekly is the rhythm promised on the page.
- **Mix:**
  - one practical observation tied to a situation, for example "what comes before the iPad battle"
  - Living Plan news
  - the opening announcement, with exact date and time, and that it's first come, first served
- Short voice notes or images are fine. No "only X left" pressure.

### Social (TikTok / Instagram)
- **Hook:** one of her scenes, in her words, in the first second (sections 7A–F).
- **Body:** show the moment with observable actions only. Then one reframe, such as «المشكلة مو إنك ما حاولتي كفاية» or «أغلب النصائح مكتوبة بشكل عام، مو لكل طفل». Then one thing to notice tomorrow.
- **CTA:** to the free assessment: «ابدئي بالتقييم المجاني، وافهمي ليش نفس المواقف تتكرر مع طفلك ومن فين تبدأي.»
- **Deep links:** use `?focus=` to link straight to her situation's landing (`sleep`, `outings`, `communication`, `food`, `transitions`, `emotions`).

## 12. Red lines checklist (run before anything goes out)

- [ ] It's about her, not the founder, and has no «أنا معاكي» in the hook.
- [ ] No therapy, sessions or specialists, and no "alongside".
- [ ] No diagnosis assumed, and no «التوحديين» in public copy.
- [ ] No promised result for the child, and no cure words.
- [ ] No blame, and no labels on the child.
- [ ] No clinical cause stated as fact. Don't use «الحساسية».
- [ ] Scarcity is real, with the true reason, no countdown and no small numbers.
- [ ] Natural Saudi Arabic that passes the read-aloud and back-translation tests.
- [ ] It isn't lukewarm. It names her real moment.

## 13. Research: what's solid, what's thin, and what to collect next

**Solid:**
- The gap: a home plan that ties everything together.
- Mothers' fatigue with tips, cure promises and programs.
- The emotional weight of visits and judgment.
- The competitor and price landscape.
- The owner's three real cases.

**Thin:** verified, word-for-word Saudi mother quotes. Saudi mothers talk on TikTok, Snapchat and WhatsApp, which web search can't reach. Several phrasings in the research are our own suggestions and must never be presented as quotes, for example «يسهر لين الفجر», «صرت أعتذر عن العزايم» and «إذا سحبت منه الآيباد تقوم القيامة». They can be used as our own descriptive wording, but test them.

**The most valuable thing to collect next:**
1. 30–50 comments from your own TikTok posts (anonymised).
2. Q15 answers from the assessment, where she describes the change she wants in her own words. These are in the Airtable results.
3. Short written feedback from the first group after each two-week review, collected with permission and with no added details.

Build a "her words" bank from these and replace the suggested phrasings with real ones.
