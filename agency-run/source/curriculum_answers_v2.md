# §0 — HOW THE CURRICULUM SECTION MUST BE FRAMED

**Read this before building the section. It changes the shape of it.**

A normal course curriculum promises a fixed sequence: *weeks 1–2 you learn this, weeks 3–4 you learn that.* That works for a course because every student gets the same week four.

**The Living Plan cannot promise that, and shouldn't try.** Its entire premise is the opposite: the milestones arrive in any order, some never arrive, others appear that nobody predicted, and the plan is explicitly built to lean into whatever is actually working rather than steering back to a predicted path. Every plan carries a section whose only job is to stop a mother measuring herself against one outcome.

Put a week-by-week timeline on the landing page and it builds the exact thing the product exists to prevent: a mother on week five who believes she is behind schedule.

### So: sequence the work, not the weeks.

Not *"by week four, fewer meltdowns."*
Instead: **three stages of work, in order, with no dates attached.**

> **أول شي — نخلي النسخة السهلة تصير بقصد**
> **بعدها — نوسعها لناس ثانية وأماكن ثانية**
> **وبعدها — ندخل اللحظة الصعبة نفسها**

Same certainty. Same clarity. Same skimmable shape. And true.

### The formula for each stage on the page

**Stage headline** = what we do together.
**Three bullets** = what she would actually see in her house if it moves in that direction — taken from that area's Directions of Growth list in §B Q1. Those lists are already written in observable language, which is exactly what the bullets need.

**One line under the three stages:** الترتيب والخطوات مبنية على طفلك أنتي.

### Why this is a selling point, not a hedge

A course gives everyone the same week four. **This gives her child the next step, decided by what actually happened in her house over the last two weeks.**

Say that plainly on the page. It is the sentence that justifies a premium price, and a fixed curriculum is structurally incapable of making the claim. The adaptivity is the product. Framed as a timeline, it reads as vagueness; framed as stages, it reads as the reason this costs more than a course.

# §A — ANSWERED

## Part 2 · Q2 — What's inside one plan

One PDF covering fourteen days. She opens it once. Nothing is drip-fed, nothing unlocks.

**Wave 1 is seven pages:** a fixed intro sheet, then six pages of her plan. From wave 2 on, six pages, straight in.

The plan always has the same five parts in the same order, so she learns the shape once:

1. **الفكرة** — one or two short paragraphs that change how she sees the situation, written from her own words about her own child. It challenges one assumption she is carrying.
2. **اتجاه النمو** — four to six observable milestones, co-created with her, wave 1 only. Not a timeline.
3. **الخطة** — the practices themselves. Either parallel daily and weekly practices, or an ordered sequence with a day-by-day table, depending on the child.
4. **ايش يعتبر تقدم** — five to seven different things that each count as progress, so she stops measuring against one outcome. At least one is about her day, not his behaviour.
5. **وإذا جا يوم صعب** — what to do when it falls apart, in four lines.

Plus **نظرة على الأسبوعين**, a single page listing the whole plan at a glance, and **ابدأي من هنا** on page one: one step she can start the day it arrives, before she has finished reading.

**Four to seven daily practices**, each attached to something already in her day — after breakfast, after pickup, before bed. **Nothing asks more than about ten minutes at a time.** Two to four weekly practices that support the daily ones rather than adding new work.

**One reflection question**, written for her specific plan, answerable in two lines.

Nothing to print, and that is deliberate. Printed cards and tracking sheets are one more thing to set up, and the mother this is built for will not set them up.

## Part 2 · Q3 — The check-in

A notification after two weeks, not a document and not inside the plan. Seven written questions, the same ones every wave regardless of focus area. They ask what changed, which step worked and what happened when she did it, which step didn't and what got in the way, whether anything showed up that nobody predicted, which of the progress signs actually happened, how her own energy compares to two weeks ago, and what she wants to work on next.

That last one is the one that matters: **she decides what the next wave is about.** Her energy answer decides how heavy the next plan is allowed to be — if she is more tired than when she started, the next wave gets lighter regardless of results.

## Part 1 · Q6 — What is never promised (all seven areas)

- That the behaviour disappears. Progress is measured several ways, never one.
- Any timeline. Not "by week four," not "within three cycles." **This is why the page uses stages, not weeks.**
- That it works the same for every child.
- Any diagnosis, assessment, or clinical claim — including about what the child feels inside.
- Anything that depends on a person she can't control: a husband who travels, a grandmother in the same house, a school.
- Catching up, closing a gap, or anything adjacent to a cure.
- Meditation or anything not in the product today.

**One more, and it's the backbone of the whole thing:** it is never implied that she caused this.

## Part 2 · Q5 — Credibility, and where it has to come from

**This is the weakest point on the page, and the curriculum section is what fixes it.**

A page this specific about outcomes invites the question of who is behind it. The usual answer is tenure — years in practice, number of clients, a credential. That answer isn't available here and must not be manufactured. An invented credential on a page where people decide whether to pay is the most damaging sentence that could appear anywhere on this site.

**So credibility comes from the method, not from the years.**

Show the method in enough detail that it is self-evidently real: the five parts, the two modes and the test that chooses between them, the fact that wave two is built from what she reported about wave one, the fact that nothing asks more than ten minutes. Specificity of this kind cannot be faked by someone who has nothing, and a reader feels that without being told.

**The curriculum section is the credential.** Build it thoroughly and the "about me" block can stay three lines, which is where it belongs anyway.

→ *What may be stated factually about background and numbers: §C. Until that is filled in, the page states nothing about either.*

## Part 1 · Q2 — The three stages, per area

**No weeks, no dates.** The same arc runs under all seven — make the easy version happen on purpose, widen it, then enter the hard moment — but each page states it in its own terms.

**Bedtime**
1. نرتب الساعة اللي قبل النوم — ترتيب ثابت، حركة بدري في اليوم، وصوت أقل آخر الليل
2. نثبت الوقت والمكان — نفس البداية كل ليلة، ونفس الطريقة مع أي أحد ينومه
3. القيام بالليل والليالي الصعبة — رد جاهز لما يقوم، وخطة لليلة اللي تنهار

**Outings & visits**
1. نبدأ بالمكان الأسهل — زيارة قصيرة، مكان يعرفه، وخروج متفق عليه
2. نطول الوقت ونوسع الأماكن — أماكن أقل ألفة، ناس أكثر، وقت أطول
3. المناسبات الطويلة واللي ما نقدر نتحكم فيها — والرد لما يتعب في النص

**Communication**
1. نفتح مواقف يحتاج فيها يطلب — نوقف الاستباق، ونعطي لحظة قبل ما نعطيه
2. ننقلها لناس ثانية — أبوه، جدته، ولد خالته، بنفس الطريقة بالضبط
3. يبدأ هو — وفي المواقف اللي يكون فيها تعبان أو متضايق

**Food**
1. نبني حول الأكل الآمن — الطاولة والجو قبل الأكل نفسه
2. نقرب الجديد بدون طلب — موجود، يشوفه، يلمسه، بدون ضغط يذوقه
3. الذوق والأماكن اللي ما نتحكم فيها — بيت الأهل، المدرسة، المطعم

**Transitions / iPad**
1. نخلي النهاية متوقعة — وقت معروف، تنبيه ثابت، وشي جاهز بعدها
2. نوحد الرد بين الكبار — وننقل نفس الطريقة لانتقالات ثانية
3. الانتقال الأصعب — ولحظة الرفض لما تصير برغم كل شي

**Anger & outbursts**
1. نخفف حمل اليوم — نوم، حركة، شاشات، ويوم متوقع أكثر
2. نشوف الإشارة قبل الانفجار — ونعطيه طريقة يعبر فيها قبلها
3. اللحظة نفسها والرجوع بعدها — رد ثابت، وطريقة ترجعون فيها لبعض

**General**
1. نبني وقت ثابت بدون طلبات — أساس يرتاح عليه اليوم كله
2. نمسك النمط — ايش يخلي اليوم الحلو حلو، ونكرره بقصد
3. نختار التحدي اللي يستاهل — ونشتغل عليه بخطة خاصة فيه

## Part 1 · Q4 — What a mother actually does in a cycle

Real examples of the *format*, from plans already produced. Swap in ones from your own past plans where you have them:

- A sentence to say five minutes before something ends — same words, same tone, every time.
- Ten minutes of play she doesn't lead, attached to a moment already in her day.
- A three-step wind-down in the same order every night. Predictability over content.
- A ready response for the moment it goes wrong: what to say, what not to give, and what to ask once he's calm.
- One agreement with the other adult in the house, so she stops being the only one saying no.
- A two-line answer to one question at the end of the week.

## Part 2 · Q8 — Real urgency

Real: a first group with an actual opening date, a founding price that genuinely rises afterwards, a cap you would actually hold. A cap is only real if you'd turn someone away at the limit — and with a manual product there is a true number, because you write every plan yourself.

Not real: a countdown that resets, "limited spots" with no number, a bonus that stays forever.
