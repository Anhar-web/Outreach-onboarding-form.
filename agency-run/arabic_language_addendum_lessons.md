ARABIC LANGUAGE FILE: ADDENDUM 2
Lessons from the Living Plan landing page: how to write Arabic copy that works the first time

Use this together with the Home Orbit Arabic Language & Voice System and Addendum 1. It applies to every kind of copy: landing pages, headlines, emails, social media posts, captions, ads and WhatsApp messages.

It records what went wrong when we wrote the Living Plan landing page, why it went wrong, and what fixed it. Read it before writing anything, so the same mistakes don't come back on a new project.
________________


1. WHAT HAPPENED

The headlines for the Living Plan landing page took three rounds to get right. Each round failed for different reasons. The page copy then needed more line-by-line corrections. Every lesson below comes from one of those corrections.
________________


2. ROUND 1: WHAT FAILED AND WHY

Example of a rejected headline:
«نفس الغضب يتكرر كل يوم؟ نبدأ من يومه كامل، مو اللحظة وحدها.»

Problems:

A. It sounded translated.
"نفس الغضب يتكرر كل يوم" reads like "The same anger repeats every day." It is grammatically fine, but nobody talks like that. It fails in Arabic and it fails in English.

B. Two half-headlines stitched together.
Every headline was the mother's scene plus our method, as if two writers who never spoke to each other had each written half.
Example: «تقولي خلاص نسكّر الآيباد وتبدأ المشكلة؟ نشوف ايش يسبقها في يومه.»
The first half is her moment. The second half jumps to a method she never asked about. The reader can't tell what the sentence is saying.

C. Vague.
She had to stop and work out what we meant. That breaks the five-second rule: within five seconds she must understand what this is and why it matters to her.

D. Lukewarm.
The lines were polite and careful, and nobody would feel anything. Never market with apathy. Use words that carry the real emotional weight of her day, like «كل ليلة نفس المعركة».

E. Self-grading.
The headlines were "scored" 85–93 out of 100. The scores meant nothing, because the lines didn't sound right when read aloud. A score never replaces the read-aloud test.

Why it happened:
The brief told the writer to put "her situation + our method" into one line. The writer followed the formula instead of writing one natural thought.

3. ROUND 2: WHAT FAILED AND WHY

Examples of rejected lines:
«كل ليلة نفس المعركة وقت النوم؟ أنا معاكي فيها.»
«أنا أنهار، والخطة الحية متابعة شهرية معاي…» (subheadline)
«افهمي ليش وقت النوم صار معركة كل ليلة، واعرفي من فين تبدأي.»
«قالوا لك طنّشيه؟ أنا أسألك ايش صار في يومه قبل ما يعصّب.»

Problems:

A. It made the page about the founder, not the mother.
«أنا معاكي» and «أنا أنهار» in the headline and subheadline put the brand at the centre. It also read like "misery loves company": I'm in your struggle with you.
Rule: the headline, the subheadline and every persuasive section are about HER. The founder never appears in the headline or subheadline. First person is allowed only where we explain how the service works, for example: «تجاوبي على أسئلة، وبعدها توصلك خطتك.» Even there, prefer what SHE does and receives.

B. "Understand" is not an outcome.
«افهمي ليش…» promises knowledge, not a change in her life. It reads like a PDF she'll get and have to figure out alone. It is lukewarm.
Rule: outcomes must be things she wants to LIVE (a calmer bedtime, a visit she enjoys), not things she will KNOW.

C. Reference examples were ignored.
The client gave Mindvalley headlines as the model. They were not studied before writing.
Rule: when references are given, break them down first. Write out what each one does, and show that analysis before writing any copy.

D. The "against ready-made advice" angle (قالوا لك…) didn't work. It was rejected.

4. ROUND 3: WHAT WORKED

Approved headlines:
«كل ليلة نفس المعركة؟ خلي وقت النوم أهدى، وروتينه أوضح، ولياليكم أخف.»
«كل عزيمة صارت همّ؟ روحي وأنتي مرتاحة، واستمتعي بوقتك مع أهلك.»
«طول اليوم وأنتي على أعصابك؟ خلي يومكم أهدى، وتعاملي مع انفعاله وأنتي عارفة ايش تسوي.»

Approved subheadline:
«خطة شخصية مبنية على يوم طفلك كامل، تركز على تحدي واحد، وتتعدل كل أسبوعين حسب استجابته، وما تضيعي شهور تجربي نصائح متفرقة لحالك.»

Why it works:
1. It starts with her challenge, in her own words, as a short question she answers "yes" to.
2. It then turns that challenge into the outcome she wants, as stacked commands (خلي… وروتينه… ولياليكم…). This follows the Mindvalley pattern: "Master your own body, age gracefully and live a life of radiant health."
3. She is the subject of every line. The founder is not mentioned.
4. It is one complete thought in one voice, so it makes sense on first read.
5. The outcomes describe direction (أهدى، أوضح، أسهل، بالتدريج), not guarantees. The copy can be bold and still honest. No promised result for the child.
6. The subheadline says what it is, how it works, and ends by removing the pain («وما تضيعي شهور تجربي نصائح متفرقة لحالك»), like "…without wasting years trying to figure it out alone."

The Mindvalley pattern, as we use it:
- The reader is the subject, always.
- Each line is an outcome she wants to live.
- Short commands, stacked.
- The subheadline gives what it is + how + removes the pain.
- The founder or teacher appears only in a dedicated section later on the page, never at the top.
- Our adaptation: start with her challenge, then move to the outcome.

5. LINE-LEVEL CORRECTIONS FROM THE CLIENT'S REVIEW

Original: «يومك كله على أعصابك؟»
Corrected: «طول اليوم وأنتي على أعصابك؟»
Why: a day can't be "on your nerves". She is.

Original: «تخمّني طول اليوم ايش يبغى؟ … وخليه يوصل لك اللي يحتاجه بطريقته»
Why rejected: «خليه يوصل لك اللي» sounds translated and off.

Original: «أغلب النصائح مكتوبة لكل الأطفال، مو لطفلك.»
Corrected: «أغلب النصائح مكتوبة بشكل عام، مو لكل طفل.»
Why: the intended idea is that advice is general and doesn't fit every child.

Original: «وأي صنف جديد على الصحن يرجع مثل ما هو.»
Corrected: «وأي أكل جديد تحطيه في صحنه يرفضه.»
Why: the original is a translated description. Say the action directly: he rejects it.

Original: «كيف تناسب الخطة طفلي، وما أحد شافه؟»
Corrected: «كيف تكون الخطة مناسبة لطفلي، والمتابعة كلها أونلاين؟»
Why: «وما أحد شافه» was unclear. Did it mean undiagnosed? Never seen by a doctor? Say exactly what is meant.

Original: «ما فيه وعد…»
Corrected: «ما في وعد…»

Original: «أنتي الحين في قائمة الانتظار»
Corrected: «أنتي دحين في قائمة الانتظار»

Original: «وهذا بالضبط اللي تسويه الخطة الحية.»
Corrected: «وهذا اللي تسويه الخطة الحية.»
Why: «بالضبط» is filler.

Original: «مو اشتراك في نصائح. هي خطة تبدأ…»
Corrected: «خطة تبدأ…»
Why: don't open by saying what it is NOT. Say what it IS.

Original: «مراجعة تفاعلية منتظمة»
Corrected: «مراجعة معاكي كل أسبوعين»
Why: «تفاعلية» is a literal translation of "interactive".

Original: «…جزء من الخطة، مو فشل لها.»
Corrected: «…جزء من الخطة، مو دليل إنها ما نفعت.»

Original: «بان إن الحساسية والضغط جزء مهم من الصورة»
Corrected: «بان إن فيه أشياء في يومه تضايقه وتضغط عليه»
Why: «الحساسية» reads as "allergy" and states a cause we can't support.

Original: a sentence starting with «بدون ما…» after a full stop
Corrected: join it with «و…» (e.g. «…حسب استجابته، وما تضيعي شهور…»)
Why: a standalone «بدون ما…» sentence is the English "…without wasting months" tail cut off.

Spelling and word preferences confirmed in this project:
دحين (not الحين) · ما في (not ما فيه) · شي · أنتي · معاكي · ايش · فين · تبدأي

6. CONTENT RULES CONFIRMED IN THIS PROJECT

1. Never mention therapy sessions, speech therapy or specialists, and never position the service as working "alongside" them. If a mother already has sessions, she can say so and the plan will take it into account. The website does not raise it.
2. Never assume the child is diagnosed. Some children are undiagnosed or too young to be diagnosed. Don't write "mothers of autistic children" in public copy.
3. Don't say "cure", "treatment for autism", or anything that implies fixing the child.
4. Don't promise results for the child. Promise what she will experience with the service: clarity, a next step, not starting from zero, not facing it alone.
5. Scarcity must be real and explained honestly. Never use countdowns or "only 3 spots left". Don't show a small waitlist number. Show demand only when it is bigger than capacity.
6. Match the copy to the stage. A waitlist page doesn't need checkout details like how to cancel a subscription.
7. Don't crowd the hero. One headline, one subheadline, one button.

7. THE WRITING PROCESS TO FOLLOW EVERY TIME

1. Study the references first. If examples are given, write a short breakdown of what each one does before writing anything. Show it.
2. Write one thought per line. If a line has two halves, check that one person could naturally say both halves in one breath. If not, rewrite it.
3. Keep her as the subject. Read every line and ask: who is this about? If the answer is the brand or the founder, rewrite it.
4. Use outcomes she lives, not knowledge she gains. Replace "understand", "learn" and "know why" with the life she wants.
5. Run the read-aloud test. Imagine a Saudi mother saying it to her sister. If she would pause, rewrite it.
6. Check for literal translation. Write a literal English back-translation under every headline and key line. If the English sounds odd AND the Arabic wouldn't be said aloud, rewrite it.
7. Run the five-second test. Can she tell what it is and why it matters to her in five seconds, without re-reading?
8. Run the emotion test. Would she feel something? If the line is safe and polite, it is probably lukewarm.
9. Don't self-score. Never present invented scores as proof of quality. The client's read-aloud judgment is the test.
10. When the meaning is unclear, ask one focused question instead of guessing.
