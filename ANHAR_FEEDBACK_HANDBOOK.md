# PART A: THE RULEBOOK

## A1. Who we're talking to and what every post must do
- **A cold new mother** who has never heard of Home Orbit, scrolling TikTok/Instagram.
- **Never say "autism" (التوحد) or any diagnosis.** Many of these children are undiagnosed, and autism is a huge umbrella. Every post names a **behavior she can see** (he covers his ears, eats only three foods, screams when the iPad is taken). No labels, no abstract concepts.
- **Every post gives real, tangible value:** steps, separate ideas, or something she can take and use. Not vague information. This applies to attention posts too.
- **No generic advice** she has heard or read a million times (timers, "be patient", "make a routine", "10 tips").
- **Every tip must be realistic** in a real, busy Saudi home, with this child, today (see the door example in B2).
- **Solve the goal she actually has**, not a workaround (bedtime = him sleeping on his own, not "how to live with lying beside him"). Name the goal; don't promise it or a timeline.
- **Don't assume she has free time.** She's been busy all day; often the only time for chores is after he sleeps.
- **She's tired and doing everything herself.** Any tip during a chore must take seconds, with zero preparation.
- **No promised results for the child, no medical/clinical causes, no therapy or specialist mentions.** (A plain hearing-check line in a "doesn't turn to his name" post was approved.) No "see a doctor" lines in food posts.
- **The client case** (waking up angry, «كان يصحى معصب…») is on the website only. **Never on social.**
- **Show the problem, then the steps.** The scene must make her feel it, but no manufactured drama and no overdramatising. Match the real size of the problem. Lukewarm ("meh") fails.

## A2. Real-life context (scenes must be realistic, not just the tips)
- **Parks** here are open grass where families sit on picnic blankets with food. Sometimes electric cars or bikes for kids. **Not amusement parks; no rides.**
- **Visits** may be to a friend's or relative's house with **no spare room**. Use only what she controls: when she arrives and leaves, where she sits, what he brings.
- **"Things being different" happens at home**, or somewhere they always visit. A child in a car doesn't know you took a different turn.
- **No cups or plates in "change" posts** (Anhar recently posted about cups). A familiar cup brought from home in a *visit* post is fine.
- **No odd changes** a mother wouldn't normally make (e.g. moving his bed).
- **People changing a routine counts as change too.** Her best change scene: his aunt doing bedtime instead of mum.
- **The iPad scream happens every time**, not just at certain times of day.
- **"Who was near him" doesn't work** as a trigger. At home it's always the same people. Use situational triggers instead: what just happened, what he was doing, where he was.

## A3. Hooks
- **One voice, start to finish.** If the two halves read like two different people wrote them without knowing about each other, rewrite the whole hook.
- **A hook, not a headline.** «كيف تساعدي طفلك…» is a headline. No jarring jump from the question to "here are X things".
- **The second half must promise something concrete she gets**: a solution, what she'll see, what to change. "Answer these three questions" is vague and promises nothing.
- **No quiz or test framing. No homework** (log, write down, record, fill a table). Nobody opens social media for homework.
- **The first line is a moment she can relive**, so precise she sees it. "Is it a phase?" is too vague: a phase of what?
- **Hand her the value; don't order her** as if she doesn't know what she's doing: «هذي ثلاث أشياء تلاحظيها…», not «المرة الجاية شوفي…».
- **No translated punctuation patterns**, e.g. a list of single words each with a question mark: «موية؟ أكل؟ آيباد؟».
- **Forced humor reads as translated.** If a joke needs explaining, cut it.
- **Naming her fear works:** «مو كل مرة يرفض فيها طفلك التغيير يعني إنه يعاندك.»

## A4. How she writes: the 12 line-level patterns
1. **Say the plain thing, not a clever English idea.** If a line needs a second to decode, it was translated. ("So he sees it coming" → «عشان ما يحس إنكم فجأة قررتوا تمشوا»)
2. **Normalise his reaction**, never make it wrong: «طبيعي ما يبغى يوقف».
3. **Use «حاولي»** when a tip depends on things outside her control: «حاولي توصلي بدري».
4. **Show why a tip works with «كذا»**: «كذا يدخل والمكان لسه هادي».
5. **Quote her own inner voice**: «وتقولي بكنس لما ينام».
6. **Show the full real scene beat by beat**: «تسحبيه، فيصرخ ويحاول ياخذه، وكل شي تعطيه بعدها يقابله بـ لا».
7. **Use a mid-slide question to make a tip land**: «تسحبي الآيباد، وعلى طول تقولي يلا شاور؟ كذا صار قدامه سبب ثاني يعترض عليه.»
8. **Prefer the everyday detail over a cute invented one** («خلص الاكل اللي في صحنه», not «آخر قطعة راحت من صحنه»).
9. **Put the subject where it belongs** («ماركة وحدة ما تتغير», not «ما يغيرها»; «لازم تكون باردة», not «تجيه»).
10. **Say tips the way she'd say them**: direct, ordered, plain verbs, the mistake first when she makes one: «لا تناديه يجي للباب عشان يلبس الجزمة. خذيها له مكانه…».
11. **Don't open a scene slide with «و».**
12. **«ممكن» is fine when it's honest**, not when it's used to soften out of fear.

- **Don't normalise spelling.** Mixed forms (ه/ة endings like الفرشه / مختلفه, جمبه/جنبه, إنتي/أنتي) stay as written so it doesn't feel too polished.
- **Spoken lines on slides use straight quotes** "…", not «…».

## A6. Carousel structure
- **It's social media, not a lecture.** People come for a solution, something valuable, or entertainment. **No walls of text**: big fonts, few words, easy to read. She should never feel she's reading a book.
- **Steps are labelled in full:** «الخطوة الأولى:»، «الخطوة الثانية:»… Non-step items name what they are: «الحساب الأول:», «أول شي:».
- **When a step mentions "the same words", give the example** («زي "يلا نوم"»).
- **Conversion posts: the bridge and CTA share ONE slide.** A separate CTA slide that repeats the bridge gets deleted. CTA = the link in bio (it goes to the free assessment).
- **Bridge formula she approved** (vary the wording):
  - «[الموقف] موقف واحد بس.»
  - «التقييم المجاني يساعدك تشوفي الصورة الأكبر وتعرفي من فين تبدئي / وتحددي أول خطوة.»
  - «الرابط في البايو.»
- **Don't list the six areas.** It reads like a brochure. «فيه مواقف تتكرر وقت النوم والأكل والطلعات وغيرها» is the most she wants.
- **Give her a reason to tap, tied back to the hook** (e.g. «…بدل ما تخمني» on a post whose hook is about guessing).

## A7. Process preferences
- **The dashboard must be a link she can open** (a published page), not a raw HTML file. A raw file showed her code or "can't open".
- **After she edits, study the edits** and turn them into rules. Don't just apply them and move on.
- **Ask about spelling or word questions in one batch**, not one by one.

# PART B: FULL HISTORY, BEFORE → AFTER, WITH HER REASONS

## B1. The brief
Her answers:
- 10 posts, **all static** (carousels): **5 attention + 5 conversion**.
- **Topics come from the research**: the market-sophistication skill plus audience research decide them.
- **Audience:** a new mother who doesn't know her yet.
- **A win** = assessment starts and waitlist sign-ups.
- **Every post must give value:** "an actionable step or steps she can take to solve a problem or get an answer to a question she has… something valuable, tangible, she can take and use. Not just vague information that doesn't really help her."
- **All conversion posts lead to the assessment**, because there's one link in bio and it goes there.
- **Value = practical steps and tips.** Nothing that promises results for the child, gives medical causes, mentions therapy, **or mentions autism**. Her reason: "many of them are undiagnosed… instead of saying the word autism, which is this huge umbrella… I decided to focus on the behavior. Something she can see, not a vague concept."

## B2. Hooks, round 1

**Dialect fixes**
- «تسوين» → **«تسوي»**. *"It might seem like Saudi speech, but «تسوي» is how I speak, and it's how the people in my area speak. So it's related to the brand voice."*
- «هالأسبوع» → **«هذا الأسبوع»**. *"I don't say it like this."*
- «الناقتس» → **«النقت»**, or switch to a different food.

**Hook 1.1 (wakes up angry): removed**

**Hook 1.2 (grandma's house)**

- Final: «يبكي ويغطي أذنه اول ما تدخلوا بيت الجدة؟ غيري طريقة الدخول نفسها، مو الزيارة.»

# PART C: VISUAL / PRODUCTION RULES (creative production, Batch 1)
- **Brand colours (official):** Deep Sea Blue **#005A70** · Ocean Teal **#00A9A5** · Pearl **#F6F3EB** · Sunrise Gold **#C9935A**.
- **Fonts:** El Messiri (headings), Tajawal (body). No other fonts (Alexandria was off-brand), no emoji.
- **Size:** 1080×1350 (4:5) for carousels.
- **No realistic visuals. They don't perform for her.** No photos, no photo+paper hybrids, no realistic food. Everything is stylised paper art.
- **Never show the child's face.**
- **Fight sameness:** recent work ("Too Much", "Leaving Grandma's") was all wide side-view room scenes with paper puppets. Vary the composition, viewpoint, scale and ground colour from post to post. The paper-art texture is the through-line, not the layout.
- **Build:** both Canva (filed in the "Home Orbit social media" folder) and rendered PNGs.
- **Blue is the primary colour.** The feed must NOT be dominated by Pearl/cream. Pearl is an accent and an occasional ground, not the default.
- **Recognisable, not identical, is non-negotiable.** "If it all looks the same, then this is another version of apathy." Open to new directions when research supports them.
- **Long slides:** when a slide is too long to stay big, split it at an existing line break or full stop (same words, one more slide). Don't shrink it into a wall of text.
- **Deliverables: PNGs only.** No PDFs, and no Canva unless she asks. She downloads the PNGs herself.
- **Documentation lives in Airtable** too: the base "Home Orbit, Social Media Agency" (appNJkIPFr6tPW6nP). Read its Feedback & Lessons, Visual Style, Posts and Post Performance tables at the start of every new batch.

# PART E: FORMATS & STRATEGY FOR BATCH 2 (Anhar, before the Batch 1 review)
- **Every post ends with a strong call to action, attention posts included.** This reverses Batch 1, where attention posts had no CTA. Why: "Our vacuum post did really well, but it didn't have a call to action, so no one actually went to the assessment."
- **Label every post's format** and vary formats across a batch, so we can compare by format. The labelled list lives in Airtable → Formats:
  - **F0 Steps:** the Batch 1 baseline.
  - **F1 HPSPC:** Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA. The default framework:
    - **Hook:** strong, specific, never mild.
    - **Problem:** made worse by going layers deep into the human "why", but not cringe.
    - **Solution:** comes right after the problem, as real actionable steps.
    - **Proof:** for the logical reader. It must be honest: no invented testimonials, and no client case on social.
    - **CTA.**
  - **F2 Story:** one moment told beat by beat.
  - **F3 Two voices:** a dialogue.
  - **F4 Metaphor:** a visual metaphor or analogy, never implying a clinical cause.
  - **F5 Walk-through:** an adapted walk-in listicle.
  - **F6 Checklist build:** self-reflection plus tension.
- **Think like Hook Point / Brendan Kane.** Storytelling over ads; formats chosen to suit the brand.
- **The four performance drivers, used to score every hook and post:**
  1. **Perspective shift:** a surprising take, not a predictable one.
  2. **Delivery and credibility:** confident, concise, no filler or over-explaining.
  3. **Hook:** short and punchy, nothing diluting it.
  4. **Self-reflection and tension:** build it up, for example with a 3-part checklist.
- **Generalist Principle:**
  - Simple words.
  - Tell it as a story.
  - A universal angle, accessible to anyone while speaking to her.
  - Turn specifics into relatable stories.
  - Use analogies.
  - Decide the **effect on the viewer** first: what should she feel, think and say to herself?
- **Proof = the method, not testimonials** (Anhar). The proof slide shows HOW Home Orbit works, in plain words:
  - We look at his whole day, not just the moment.
  - We work on one area at a time.
  - The plan adjusts to how he responds, reviewed every two weeks.
  - It's built from her own observations.
  - The free assessment looks at six areas of his day.
  
  No invented testimonials. The client case stays off social.
- **A real audience comment, on an OLD problem-only post that flopped** (problem → more problem → more problem → CTA, with no tool or steps):
  > «عزيزتي نصيحة اذا تريدي الناس يشتركو معك او يشترو المنتج الي عارضتيه لازم تعطينا رؤوس اقلام تخلي الواحد يحكم ان الكلام يستاهل الشراء، مو تطرحي المشكلة وخلاص طريقتك غير مقنعة ابداً تقبلي نقدي وتحياتي»
  
  The lesson in the audience's own words: never post problem-only content. Every post gives «رؤوس أقلام», meaning concrete key points (steps, the tool, the method), so she can judge it's worth it **before** the CTA.
- **F5 Walking listicle** (refined from a Hook Point talk Anhar shared, Robert Croke's example):
  - **Hook:** her desired result, asked as a question, plus "here are my 3 best…".
  - **Points:** one numbered point per slide, short and confident.
  - **The twist:** each point has one that is **unexpected but true** and breaks advice she's heard before. It's never against her or the child, and never a clinical claim.
  - **Close:** fast and energetic, then the CTA.
  - Easy to produce. On a carousel, swiping replaces walking, so cut every filler slide.
